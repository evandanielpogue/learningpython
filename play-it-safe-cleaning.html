Why it matters

Play equipment is the highest-touch surface you own — and the only one on nobody's cleaning schedule

Hands, then mouths

Handrails, grab bars, steering wheels and slide walls collect everything a toddler's hands carry. Indoor high-touch points get wiped daily. Outdoor ones get rained on.

Bird droppings and mold

Shaded decks and tube slides hold moisture. What grows there is a slip hazard before it is a health one, and it stains permanently if left through a season.

Parents notice first

A grimy play structure is the thing a touring parent photographs. For a childcare center, clean equipment is enrollment marketing that also happens to be sanitation.

What a visit looks like

Four steps, in this order, every time

The sequence is not arbitrary. Disinfectant does not work on a dirty surface, and it needs contact time before it is rinsed.

  1. 01

    Walk and document

    Debris pickup, and a visual safety pass — loose bolts, protruding hardware, worn S-hooks, surfacing gaps under swings and slide exits. Anything found goes in your report with a photo. We clean; we do not repair.

  2. 02

    Soft wash

    Low pressure and a biodegradable detergent, applied with soft-bristle brushes on plastics, powder-coated steel and painted metal. ≤500 PSI on play components — enough to lift grime, not enough to etch plastic or strip coating.

  3. 03

    Sanitize with dwell time

    An EPA-registered disinfectant rated for child-occupied spaces, held on high-touch points for the full contact time printed on the label. No undiluted bleach, ever — it corrodes metal, degrades rubber and plastic, and leaves residue that irritates skin.

  4. 04

    Rinse, dry, report

    Clean-water rinse, air dry, then a before/after photo set and the safety notes emailed to you the same day. Equipment is back in service as soon as it is dry.

Surfaces

What we do — and deliberately don't do — to each surface

SurfaceOur methodWhat we never do
Plastic & rotomold components Soft wash, soft-bristle brush, microfiber detail on touch points High-pressure tips that chalk and dull the finish
Powder-coated & painted steel Low pressure, pH-neutral detergent, immediate rinse Abrasives or bleach that undercut the coating
Poured-in-place rubber (PIP) Blower and nylon brush; vinegar-and-water dwell on stains, gentle scrub, rinse Never pressure washed. Never metal bristles.
Engineered wood fiber Rake, de-compact, level to depth at swing and slide exits; flag when top-off is due Wetting it down — saturated fiber compacts harder
Wood structures Controlled wash with the nozzle always moving 1,200–1,500 PSI Standing in one spot, which furs the grain and raises splinters
Shade sails & canopies Rinse and spot-treat mildew by hand Anything that stresses the seams or hardware

Poured-in-place surfacing costs more than the structure standing on it. A cleaner who arrives with a 3,000 PSI wand and no plan for it is a liability, not a bargain.

FAQ

Questions we get on every first call

Is what you spray safe for children?

We use EPA-registered disinfectants rated for use in child-occupied spaces, applied at label dilution and rinsed. We do not use undiluted bleach — beyond being hard on skin, it corrodes metal fasteners and degrades rubber and plastic components over time.

Safety data sheets for every product are yours on request. If your center or district has an approved products list, send it and we will work from it.

How long until kids can play again?

As soon as the equipment is dry, typically 30 to 60 minutes after we finish depending on weather and shade. We schedule around your day — before open, during nap, or on a weekend — so in practice there is usually no downtime at all.

Will pressure washing damage our equipment or surfacing?

It absolutely can, which is why we mostly do not use it. Play components get soft washing at low pressure with brushes. Wood gets controlled pressure with the nozzle always moving.

Poured-in-place rubber surfacing never gets pressure washed at all — the granules dislodge and the top layer tears, which reduces how well it cushions a fall. That is a safety-rated surface, not a patio, and it is the single most common way a playground gets damaged by a well-meaning cleaner.

What does it cost?

Most single-structure sites land between $225 and $595 per visit depending on scope, size and surfacing. Recurring schedules cost meaningfully less per visit than one-time calls, and multiple properties routed together cost less again.

We quote a fixed per-visit price after walking the site. No hourly billing, no surprises on the invoice.

How often do we actually need this?

Quarterly is the minimum we recommend for any regularly used site. Licensed childcare and heavy-traffic equipment is usually better on monthly or every-other-week — at that cadence each visit is faster and cheaper, because grime never gets established.

Climate matters. Shaded, humid sites grow mold on tube slides and under decks far faster than open sunny ones.

Are you insured, and can you pass our vendor approval?

Yes. We carry general liability and abuse & molestation coverage, and we can name your organization as additional insured. Technicians are background checked, and fingerprinted where a district requires it for contractors with campus access.

Send us your vendor packet and we will fill it out. We have been through the process and it does not slow us down.

Do you inspect for safety problems too?

We document. Every visit includes a visual pass for the obvious hazards — loose or protruding hardware, worn swing hangers, cracks, surfacing thin at fall zones — photographed and noted in your report.

That is a cleaner's documentation, not a certified inspection. If something needs a credentialed inspector or a parts repair, we tell you plainly and point you to someone who does it.

What about engineered wood fiber and mulch surfacing?

Loose-fill surfacing compacts and migrates, especially under swings and at slide exits, which is exactly where depth matters. We rake, de-compact and re-level it as part of a visit, and we measure depth and tell you when a top-off is due — generally every two to three years.

We have several properties. Can you do all of them?

That is our favorite call. Multiple sites scheduled as a route cost less per site and get one consolidated report. Property managers, multi-site childcare operators and districts with several campuses should ask about route pricing.
