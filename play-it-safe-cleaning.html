Why it matters

It's the highest-touch surface on your property — and it's on nobody's cleaning schedule

Indoor high-touch points get wiped down every day. Outdoor ones just get rained on, and rain doesn't sanitize anything.

🤲

Every hand, all day

Handrails, grab bars, steering wheels and slide walls get gripped a few hundred times between morning drop-off and pickup. Nothing else on your property takes that kind of traffic.

🦠

What grows in the shade

Tube slides and shaded decks hold moisture all season long. Mold and algae are a slip hazard before they're a health one, and they stain permanently if you leave them.

👀

Parents notice first

A grimy structure is the thing a touring parent quietly photographs. Clean equipment is enrollment marketing that happens to also be sanitation.

How a visit works

Four steps, in this order, every single time

The order isn't for show. Disinfectant does nothing on a dirty surface, and it needs its full contact time on the equipment before anything gets rinsed.

STEP 1

Walk & document

We clear debris and do a visual safety pass — loose bolts, protruding hardware, worn S-hooks, thin surfacing at swing and slide exits. Anything we find gets photographed into your report.

STEP 2

Soft wash

Low pressure and biodegradable detergent, applied with soft-bristle brushes on plastics, powder coat and painted steel. Enough to lift grime, never enough to etch or strip.

≤ 500 PSI on play components
STEP 3

Sanitize with dwell

EPA-registered disinfectant rated for spaces children occupy, held on every touch point for the full label contact time. Never undiluted bleach — it eats metal, degrades rubber and leaves residue kids react to.

EPA List N products
STEP 4

Rinse & report

Clean-water rinse, air dry, then before-and-after photos and the safety notes land in your inbox the same day — whether or not anyone was on site to meet us.

Back in service when dry

FAQ

Questions we get on every first call

Is what you spray actually safe for children?

Yes. We use EPA-registered disinfectants rated for spaces children occupy, mixed at label dilution and rinsed afterward. No harsh chemicals, no residue left behind for little hands to find.

We don't use undiluted bleach. Beyond being rough on skin, it corrodes metal fasteners and breaks down rubber and plastic over time. Safety data sheets for every product are yours on request, and if your center keeps an approved products list, send it over and we'll work from it.

How long until the kids can play again?

As soon as it's dry — usually 30 to 60 minutes after we finish, depending on weather and shade. Because we schedule around your day, most of our customers never lose a minute of playtime.

Will pressure washing damage our equipment or surfacing?

It absolutely can, which is exactly why we mostly don't use it. Play components get soft washing at low pressure with brushes. Wood gets controlled pressure with the nozzle always moving.

Poured-in-place rubber never gets pressure washed at all. The granules dislodge and the top layer tears, and that layer is what cushions a fall. It's safety-rated surfacing, not a patio — and it's the single most common way a playground gets wrecked by a well-meaning cleaner.

How does pricing work?

Every site is different, so we quote after a free walkthrough rather than guessing from a photo. Footprint, surfacing type, how much equipment you have and how easy it is to get a truck near it all move the number.

You get a flat per-visit price up front — no hourly billing and no surprises on the invoice. Recurring schedules cost less per visit than one-off calls, and multiple properties routed together cost less again.

How often do we actually need this?

Quarterly is the minimum we'd recommend anywhere children play regularly. Licensed childcare and heavy-traffic equipment usually does better monthly or every other week — and at that cadence each visit is faster, because grime never gets a chance to establish.

Climate matters too. Shaded, humid sites grow mold in tube slides and under decks far faster than open sunny ones.

Are you insured, and can you pass our vendor approval?

Yes to both. We carry general liability and abuse & molestation coverage, and we can name your organization as additional insured. Technicians are background checked, and fingerprinted where a district requires it for contractors with campus access.

Send us your vendor packet and we'll fill it out. We've been through the process before and it won't slow your start date down.

Do you inspect for safety problems too?

We document. Every visit includes a visual pass for the obvious hazards — loose or protruding hardware, worn swing hangers, cracks, surfacing gone thin at fall zones — photographed and noted in your report.

That's a cleaner's documentation, not a certified inspection. If something needs a credentialed inspector or a parts repair, we'll tell you straight and point you to someone who does that work.

What about engineered wood fiber and mulch?

Loose-fill surfacing compacts and migrates, especially under swings and at slide exits — exactly where depth matters most. We rake it, break up the compaction and re-level as part of a visit, then measure depth and let you know when a top-off is due, generally every two to three years.

We have several properties. Can you handle all of them?

That's our favorite phone call. Multiple sites scheduled as a route cost less per site and come back as one consolidated report. Property managers, multi-site childcare operators and districts with several campuses should definitely ask about route scheduling.

What do you need from us to get started?

An address, your surfacing type if you happen to know it, and a rough sense of how often you'd like us out. We walk the site, send a plan and a fixed quote, and the first visit is usually within a couple of weeks.
