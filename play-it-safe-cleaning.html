The problem

It's the highest-touch surface you own, and it's on nobody's cleaning schedule

🤲

Hands, then mouths

Handrails, grab bars, steering wheels and slide walls collect everything a toddler's hands carry. Indoor touch points get wiped daily. Outdoor ones get rained on.

🦠

Mold in the shade

Tube slides and shaded decks hold moisture all season. What grows in there is a slip hazard before it's a health one — and it stains permanently if left.

👀

Touring parents notice

A grimy structure is the thing a prospective parent photographs. For a childcare center, a clean playground is enrollment marketing that happens to be sanitation.

How a visit works

Four steps, in this order, every single time

The order isn't arbitrary — disinfectant does nothing on a dirty surface, and it needs its full contact time before it's rinsed.

STEP 1

Walk & document

Debris clear plus a visual safety pass: loose bolts, protruding hardware, worn S-hooks, thin surfacing at swing and slide exits. Everything found is photographed into your report.

STEP 2

Soft wash

Low pressure and biodegradable detergent with soft-bristle brushes on plastics, powder coat and painted steel. Enough to lift grime, not enough to etch or strip.

≤ 500 PSI on play components
STEP 3

Sanitize with dwell

EPA-registered disinfectant rated for child-occupied spaces, held on touch points for the full label contact time. Never undiluted bleach — it eats metal, degrades rubber, irritates skin.

EPA List N products
STEP 4

Rinse & report

Clean-water rinse, air dry, then before/after photos and the safety notes in your inbox the same day — whether or not anyone was there to meet us.

Back in service when dry

Surfaces

What we do — and refuse to do — to each surface

SurfaceOur methodWhat we never do
Plastic & rotomold Soft wash, soft-bristle brush, microfiber on touch points High-pressure tips that chalk and dull the finish
Powder-coated steel Low pressure, pH-neutral detergent, immediate rinse Abrasives or bleach that undercut the coating
Poured-in-place rubber Blower and nylon brush; vinegar-and-water dwell on stains, gentle scrub, rinse Never pressure washed. Never metal bristles.
Engineered wood fiber Rake, de-compact and level to depth at fall zones; flag when top-off is due Soaking it — saturated fiber compacts harder
Wood structures Controlled wash, nozzle always moving, 1,200–1,500 PSI Dwelling in one spot, which furs grain and raises splinters
Shade sails Rinse and hand spot-treat mildew Anything that stresses seams or hardware

Your poured-in-place surfacing cost more than the structure standing on it. A cleaner who shows up with a 3,000 PSI wand and no plan for it is a liability, not a bargain.

FAQ

Questions we get on every first call

Is what you spray safe for children?

We use EPA-registered disinfectants rated for child-occupied spaces, applied at label dilution and rinsed. We don't use undiluted bleach — beyond being hard on skin, it corrodes metal fasteners and degrades rubber and plastic over time.

Safety data sheets for every product are yours on request. If your center or district keeps an approved products list, send it and we'll work from it.

How long until kids can play again?

As soon as it's dry — typically 30 to 60 minutes after we finish, depending on weather and shade. We schedule around your day, so in practice there's usually no downtime at all.

Will pressure washing damage our equipment or surfacing?

It absolutely can, which is why we mostly don't use it. Play components get soft washing at low pressure with brushes. Wood gets controlled pressure with the nozzle always moving.

Poured-in-place rubber never gets pressure washed at all — granules dislodge and the top layer tears, which reduces how well it cushions a fall. That's a safety-rated surface, not a patio, and it's the single most common way a playground gets wrecked by a well-meaning cleaner.

What does it cost?

Most single-structure sites land between $225 and $595 per visit depending on scope, size and surfacing. Recurring schedules cost meaningfully less per visit than one-off calls, and multiple properties routed together cost less again.

We quote a fixed per-visit price after walking the site. No hourly billing, no surprises on the invoice.

How often do we actually need this?

Quarterly is the minimum we'd recommend for any regularly used site. Licensed childcare and heavy-traffic equipment usually does better monthly or every other week — at that cadence each visit is faster and cheaper, because grime never gets established.

Climate matters too. Shaded, humid sites grow mold on tube slides and under decks far faster than open sunny ones.

Are you insured, and can you pass our vendor approval?

Yes. We carry general liability and abuse & molestation coverage, and we can name your organization as additional insured. Technicians are background checked, and fingerprinted where a district requires it for contractors with campus access.

Send us your vendor packet and we'll fill it out. We've been through the process and it doesn't slow us down.

Do you inspect for safety problems too?

We document. Every visit includes a visual pass for the obvious hazards — loose or protruding hardware, worn swing hangers, cracks, thin surfacing at fall zones — photographed and noted in your report.

That's a cleaner's documentation, not a certified inspection. If something needs a credentialed inspector or a parts repair, we tell you plainly and point you to someone who does it.

What about engineered wood fiber and mulch?

Loose-fill surfacing compacts and migrates, especially under swings and at slide exits — exactly where depth matters. We rake, de-compact and re-level as part of a visit, measure depth, and tell you when a top-off is due, generally every two to three years.

We have several properties. Can you do all of them?

That's our favorite call. Multiple sites scheduled as a route cost less per site and get one consolidated report. Property managers, multi-site childcare operators and districts with several campuses should ask about route pricing.

What do you need from us to get started?

An address, your surfacing type if you know it, and a rough idea of how often you want us. We walk the site, send a fixed quote, and the first visit is usually within two weeks.
