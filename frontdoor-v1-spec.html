V1 Specification · Platform

Run your job search like a named account.

A self-serve platform that turns one job listing into a five-person org map, a fourteen-day multithreaded sequence, and a public account plan addressed to that company. Built first for SaaS account executives, who already speak the language.

What makes it a platform

Four things separate this from a template library with AI bolted on

Contact lookup is already commoditized — DearHiringManager, FindHR, ContactHire and Jobifer all sell "paste a job URL, get the hiring manager." None of them have any of the following.

01

An outcome feedback loop

Every touch has a logged outcome: no reply, reply, meeting, referral. That data attaches to the skill, persona, channel, seniority and company type that produced it. Within a few thousand campaigns you know which opener works on a VP Sales at a Series B, and nobody else does. This is the only genuinely defensible asset in the product.

02

User-owned, forkable skills

Skills are visible, editable instruction packs, not hidden prompts. A user's edits persist across campaigns and compound into a personal playbook. Eight tuned skills is a switching cost. Publishing them is a marketplace.

03

A public artifact that markets itself

The account-plan page is the only surface a hiring manager sees. Every VP Sales who opens one is a future customer or referrer. That is why the page ships free and unbranded upsells are the paywall, not the page itself.

04

A second side of the market

Career coaches, outplacement firms and sales bootcamps buy seats, and — unlike the job seeker — they do not churn when the candidate gets hired. That is the answer to a customer base whose success condition is cancelling.

Seed data is the bootstrap problem

The feedback loop is worthless at zero campaigns, and it is the thing that makes this defensible. So the first ten campaigns run through the real platform, with the founder doing research and review behind the seams — concierge onboarding on top of working software, not an agency. Those ten produce the first outcome rows, the first reply-rate benchmark, and the marketing number everything else is sold on.

The engine

Outcome capture is the product's spine

Most job tools end at "here's your message." The value compounds only if the platform learns what happened next — which means capturing outcomes has to be nearly free for the user.

How outcomes get captured

  • Page views — the page fires an event when opened. Zero user effort, and the strongest signal in the system.
  • One-tap logging — each scheduled touch shows three buttons: no reply / replied / meeting booked.
  • Day-14 debrief — one screen at campaign close: what happened, which message got the reply, paste it back.

What it feeds

  • Per-skill reply rates, sliced by persona, seniority, channel and company stage
  • Automatic reordering of the sequence when a variant beats the default
  • The public benchmark — "campaigns on Frontdoor get a 34% reply rate" — which is the entire marketing engine
  • Skill marketplace ranking, so the best published packs surface on merit

Store queries, not people

Contacts are resolved at query time through a licensed provider, rendered to the user, and discarded. Do not warehouse names, employers and titles. LinkedIn's anti-scraping terms are enforceable as contract claims — hiQ Labs ended with a $500,000 judgment, a permanent injunction, and an order to delete all code and data. Separately, a California company holding a database of personal information it did not collect first-party may qualify as a data broker under the Delete Act: $6,000 annual registration, DROP checks every 45 days, and $200 per day per consumer in penalties. Outcome rows reference a hashed contact key and a role, never a person.

Architecture

Object model

Campaign is the unit of everything — pricing, UI, and measurement. One campaign is one user, one company, one role. That constraint is the product.

User
 ├─ VoiceProfile      // from conversational onboarding; user-visible and editable
 ├─ Resume            // parsed to structured claims, not just a stored PDF
 ├─ SkillOverride[]   // the user's edits; persist across every campaign
 └─ Campaign
     ├─ Company       // research brief + a point of view to react to
     ├─ Role          // pasted JD, parsed to requirements
     ├─ Contact[]     // max 5, one per persona, resolved at query time
     ├─ Play[]        // skill instance = Skill + VoiceProfile + Contact + Company
     ├─ Touch[]       // scheduled send, channel, status, outcome
     └─ Page          // public account plan, one per campaign

Skill {
  id, name, persona, stage, channel, default_day
  objective       // the single job this message does
  principles[]    // rules the play obeys — shown to the user
  structure       // opening / middle / ask
  constraints     // word cap, one ask, banned phrases
  examples[]      // good + bad, annotated
  stats           // reply rate, meeting rate, n — from the loop
}

Conversational onboarding

Six questions in a chat interface, about four minutes. This is a discovery call, not a form — it produces the raw material every downstream message depends on. Intent-adaptive conversational onboarding shows roughly 3.2× median activation improvement over fixed tours, but input quality is the real reason here.

  1. What role are you going after?
  2. Walk me through the win you're proudest of.
  3. What do you do that most people in your role don't?
  4. Paste a message you've sent that you were proud of.
  5. Peer, expert, or eager — how do you want to come across?
  6. Anything you don't want said about you?

Question 4 is the highest-value input in the product. Show the resulting VoiceProfile back to the user — "here's how I'll write as you" is the activation moment.

The page

Not a portfolio. A one-page account plan addressed to one company — the job-search equivalent of a mutual action plan.

  • Name, target role, one-line thesis
  • Why [Company] — the point of view
  • Three proof points with numbers
  • A 30-60-90 for this role (a generic one defeats the purpose)
  • 60–90 second video — embed Loom or YouTube, don't host
  • Resume download and contact

Render from a DB row on a dynamic route, never statically generated per user. On Cloudflare, static egress is free and unmetered and Workers Paid is $5/month; at ten thousand users this is tens of dollars, not a cost center.

Peer first, hiring manager third

No good account executive opens an account by calling the CEO. You develop a coach, gather intel, then go up with something to say. This ordering is the clearest signal in the product that it was built by someone who has actually run a territory — and teaching users why the order is the order is the education layer doing real work.

Sequencing

Build order

Weeks 1–3

The spine

Auth, onboarding chat → VoiceProfile, resume parse, JD parse, campaign creation, the page renderer with view events. No contact lookup yet — the user enters contacts manually. Ship this and it is already usable.

Weeks 4–5

Skills engine

The twelve skills as data, the composer (Skill + VoiceProfile + Contact + Company), the editable skill view, the two sliders, and the annotation layer. Generation is the easy part; the schema is the product.

Week 6

Sequence and outcomes

The fourteen-day scheduler, one-tap outcome logging, the day-14 debrief, and the stats pipeline. Nothing auto-sends — the user sends from their own email and LinkedIn, which dodges deliverability, platform terms and spam liability, and produces better outreach anyway.

Weeks 7–8

Contact resolution and the first ten

Licensed provider integration, query-time only. Then run ten real campaigns through the platform with concierge support behind the seams to seed the outcome data.

Pricing

  • Free — 1 campaign, 3 contacts, branded page. The distribution loop.
  • $99 per campaign — 5 contacts, full sequence, all skills, unbranded page, 30 days.
  • $199 for three — the real product. Nobody targets one company.
  • $29/mo — custom domain, pages stay live, skill library persists.

Priced against a $150k role, not against Teal's $29/month. Freemium conversion runs about 4% in normal SaaS and worse in job tools, and the customer's success condition is cancelling — so campaign pricing, not subscription, carries the business.

Cut from V1

  • Auto-send of any kind
  • LinkedIn integration
  • Video hosting
  • Job board scraping — the user pastes the JD
  • Mobile app
  • Coach and team dashboards (that is V2, and it is the business)
  • Resume rewriting — Teal's game, a race to zero

Kill criteria

Ten campaigns. If reply rate across touches is under 15% and fewer than three campaigns produce a first conversation, the thesis is wrong and you found out for the price of two weekends. If reply rate clears 30% and one in three campaigns produces an interview, you have a product and its entire marketing message in the same number.
